# General-purpose review platforms for dealerships

These are not automotive-specific (unlike `car-sites.md`'s DealerRater/Cars.com/CarGurus dealer ratings) — they're general local-business review platforms that happen to cover car dealerships alongside every other kind of business. Useful as a second opinion on a dealership's reputation, not for vehicle search or vehicle reviews.

| Site | What it's good for | Caveats |
|---|---|---|
| [Google Reviews](https://www.google.com/maps) | Highest review volume of any platform for most dealerships; ties directly into Google Search/Maps results. Good first stop. | Susceptible to review-gating and incentivized reviews like any platform; check the *number* of reviews, not just the star average. |
| [Yelp](https://www.yelp.com) | Independent audience from Google's; Yelp's filtering algorithm is stricter about suspected fake reviews. | Lower review volume for dealerships specifically than Google or automotive-specific sites — car buyers are less likely to think to check Yelp first. |
| [Facebook](https://www.facebook.com) (Page recommendations/reviews) | Useful when a dealership is more active on Facebook than elsewhere; can surface comments/photos tied to real transactions. | Reviews are tied to real Facebook identities, which cuts both ways — more accountable, but some people won't leave a public review under their real name. |
| [Better Business Bureau (BBB)](https://www.bbb.org) | A+ to F letter-grade rating, plus a dedicated **complaints tab** showing filed complaints and how the dealership resolved them — good for checking for a pattern of unresolved disputes. | The letter grade and the complaints tab can disagree significantly (a dealership can hold an A+ while having many recent complaints) — always check the complaints tab directly, don't rely on the grade alone. |
| [Trustpilot](https://www.trustpilot.com) | Companies aren't allowed to pay to hide or remove reviews, which makes it harder to scrub bad ones. | Lower adoption among car dealerships specifically than among e-commerce/retail businesses — a dealership with no Trustpilot presence isn't unusual. |

## How to use these alongside `car-sites.md`

Automotive-specific dealer reviews (DealerRater, Cars.com, CarGurus, KBB) come from people who went through the car-buying process specifically and tend to be more detailed about salespeople, pricing transparency, and financing experience. General platforms (this file) are useful as a cross-check — a dealership can curate what shows up on its own listings pages more easily than it can control every general review site, so a big gap between "spotless on Cars.com" and "poor on Google/BBB" is itself a signal worth noticing.

Recommended order: check the automotive-specific dealer rating first (from `car-sites.md`), then cross-check Google Reviews (highest volume) and the BBB complaints tab (best for spotting unresolved disputes) before treating the dealership as vetted.
