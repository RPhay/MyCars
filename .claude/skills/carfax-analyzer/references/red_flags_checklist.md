# Expert red-flag checklist

Compiled from how dealer inspectors, appraisers, and mechanics read vehicle history reports.

## Title
- Any brand at all (see `title_brands.md`) — salvage, junk, and flood are the highest severity.
- Title issued in a different state than where major damage/accident was reported (a sign of "title washing" — re-titling in a state with weaker branding laws to shed a brand).
- "Not actual mileage" or "exceeds mechanical limits" notations.

## Odometer
- Any reading lower than a previously recorded reading (rollback indicator).
- Large unexplained jump in mileage between two close dates.
- Long gap (2+ years) with no recorded reading, especially right before a sale.
- Implausibly low mileage for the vehicle's age (well under ~6-8k miles/year sustained) with no clear explanation (e.g. garage-kept collector car with matching service history).
- Reported mileage in the history inconsistent with the physical odometer/dashboard at time of purchase — flag for physical verification, report can't confirm this itself.

## Accidents / damage
- Any airbag deployment.
- "Structural" or "frame" damage language, as opposed to purely cosmetic/bumper damage.
- Multiple accidents, even if each is individually minor.
- An accident recorded shortly before a sale (possible repaired-to-flip pattern).
- Severe damage with no corresponding salvage title (may mean damage was under the total-loss threshold but still structurally significant, or was paid out-of-pocket and never brought to an insurer/inspector).

## Ownership pattern
- Many owners in a short overall timeframe.
- Any single ownership period under ~1 year (quick flips suggest the seller found a problem).
- Ownership length that's very short right after a major accident or major repair record.
- Fleet, rental, taxi, rideshare, police, or government use — higher wear per mile than personal use.
- Lease-return vehicles are lower risk than rental/fleet but still see more varied drivers.

## Service & maintenance
- No service records at all for a vehicle of significant age/mileage.
- Long gaps between service visits relative to the vehicle's mileage accumulation rate.
- A major repair (transmission, engine, etc.) recorded shortly before the current sale listing, with no clear reason given — can indicate a seller patching a known problem just enough to sell.
- Sparse records that only ever show oil changes, never anything else, on a 10+ year old car.

## Recalls
- Any open (not-completed) manufacturer recall — free to fix, but the vehicle should not be purchased/driven without a plan to complete it immediately, especially airbag/steering/brake recalls.

## Report-level alerts
- Carfax (or equivalent) "potential odometer rollback," "problem detected," "issue found," or similar system-generated alerts — always investigate the underlying data line, don't just relay the badge.
- "Buyback guarantee eligible" language implies the provider found no branding at time of report generation — this is a coverage statement, not a guarantee of vehicle condition.

## What the report cannot show (always disclose)
- Accidents or damage never reported to insurance, police, or a data-sharing shop (cash repairs are invisible).
- Mechanical condition (engine/transmission health, wear items) — reports document *events*, not current condition.
- Independent/backyard-shop service work that doesn't report to data aggregators.
- Anything that happened very recently and hasn't yet propagated into the data source.

A report with zero flags is evidence of no *reported* issues — not proof of a mechanically sound vehicle. Always recommend a pre-purchase inspection by an independent mechanic and a physical odometer/VIN check regardless of how clean the report looks.
