# Title brand definitions

A "brand" is a permanent, state-issued notation on a vehicle's title. Once applied, it cannot be removed by re-registration or a change of state (though some states historically "washed" brands — treat an unbranded title on a vehicle with a branding history in another state as a red flag itself).

- **Salvage** — insurer declared the vehicle a total loss (damage cost exceeded a state-set percentage of value). Not legally drivable until rebuilt and re-inspected.
- **Rebuilt / Reconstructed** — a former salvage vehicle that has been repaired and passed a state safety re-inspection. Legal to drive and sell, but the salvage event behind it (cause, severity) should still be examined.
- **Flood** — history of significant water damage. High risk: corrosion, electrical/sensor failure, mold, can surface months to years later.
- **Fire** — history of fire damage.
- **Hail** — cosmetic/body damage from hailstorm; mechanically low-risk but affects resale value.
- **Lemon / Manufacturer Buyback** — manufacturer repurchased the vehicle under state lemon law because a defect could not be fixed after repeated attempts. The underlying defect may recur; ask which component and how many repair attempts.
- **Junk** — sold for parts/scrap; should not be titled for road use. Treat any road-use offer of a junk-branded vehicle as disqualifying.
- **Not Actual Mileage** — odometer reading is known to not reflect true mileage (rollback, broken odometer, or replaced cluster with unknown offset).
- **Exceeds Mechanical Limits** — mechanical odometer maxed out and rolled over; true mileage is higher than displayed.

Treat any brand as a hard flag warranting an independent inspection at minimum; salvage/flood/junk/lemon warrant the most caution.
